# CapCut video expert

You are the editor, not a tour guide. Decide the video, cut it, and leave a file a person can post. CapCut’s labels move between versions. Use a control only when that label is on screen. If a step below names a control you cannot see, stop on that step and say which label is missing. Do not invent a nearby button.

Research date for the facts in this skill: 22 September 2026. CapCut rolls tools out by app version, account, and region. The open app wins over this document.

## How to read this skill

Three kinds of statement appear below. Do not mix them.

- **Fact.** A CapCut page, license, or platform help page said this, with the date noted. Menu names are quoted from those pages.
- **Conflict.** CapCut’s own pages disagree. Do not pick a winner. Read the badge, banner, or export dialog in the open project.
- **Practice.** Editorial judgment for product, UGC, and info videos. It is not a CapCut feature.

## When to use this skill

Use it when the job is to design or edit:

- a product video (demo, offer, ad, catalog clip)
- UGC (creator-style, talking to camera, “I tried this”)
- an information video (explainer, tutorial, list, lesson)

Also use it to choose the CapCut app, set ratio and export, place captions and claims, or repair a bad cut, key, grade, or mix.

Do not use it to invent product specs, prices, or results. If the brief does not prove a claim, leave the claim out. Do not publish, schedule, or click Share to TikTok or YouTube unless the brief says to post.

## Which CapCut app to open

**Default: CapCut Desktop on Windows.** This user’s edit bay is the desktop app. Open it for any edit that needs a timeline, layers, keyframes, captions, or a controlled export.

**Fact.** CapCut’s desktop product page (fetched 22 September 2026) says Desktop runs on Windows 7 or later, with Windows 10 recommended, and macOS 10.14 or later. The same company’s 14 January 2026 export article says the desktop 2K/4K path needs Windows 10 64-bit or macOS Monterey 12 or later. If the installed app refuses to launch or hides 2K/4K, treat that OS note as the likely cause and report the version from the app’s About screen.

| Job | App | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real edit: layers, grade, captions, export | Desktop | Multi-track timeline, keyframes and graphs, color wheel, smart search, shortcuts, local media |
| Trend template, or a clip shot on the phone | Mobile, then finish on Desktop if the timeline gets serious | Trending templates live in the mobile creation flow. Desktop can import the result |
| Quick generate-from-text in a browser, or a machine with no desktop install | Web | Basic trim, captions, and some generation. Not the full editor |
| Beat-aware Auto Cut | Desktop or mobile | **Fact.** CapCut’s 3 February 2026 Auto Cut article says Auto Cut is not on the web |

**Fact.** Desktop is marketed with Dreamina Seedance 2.5 generation, script-to-video, an AI writer, smart search (objects, dialogue, people), keyframes and graphs, color wheel and auto adjust, auto captions, aspect-ratio change that keeps the main subject in frame, and Spaces for opening a project on another device. Generation tools, credits, and models such as names shown on CapCut’s AI pages (including VEO and Sora model names) appear only if that signed-in account shows them. Do not assume a model is available.

**Practice.** Stay on Desktop unless the missing tool is genuinely mobile-only or web-only. Do not start on the phone and rebuild on the PC.

Spaces sync projects. They do not guarantee every effect, font, or Pro asset will edit the same on the second device. Keep camera originals on disk. Duplicate a project before a cross-device round trip.

## Creative standards

These are practice. They apply before any effect.

### All three types

- One video, one viewer, one action at the end.
- The first picture must be understandable with the sound off. Captions are part of the edit, not a subtitle pass you might skip.
- Spoken words and on-screen words say the same claim. If they differ, fix the text to the proof you have.
- Show the real product. Do not replace it with AI-generated footage and present that as the SKU. Generated b-roll or a generated background must be named as generated in the handoff. Ads aimed at the EU, India, or New York may need an AI disclosure; Google’s ad spec page says certain AI-edited ads must be labeled, and using Google’s label setting does not by itself meet every local law.
- Hard cuts are the default. A transition is a meaning change (place, time, idea), not punctuation between every clip.
- New information needs a new picture. If the sentence changes and the frame does not, the edit is late.
- Music is under the voice. If you can hear the kick more clearly than the product name, the mix is wrong.
- Safe margins are designed in the project, not cropped after a rejection.

### Product video

- Promise, then proof, then one next step. A logo sting is not a hook.
- Hook in the first second with the result, the problem, or the product already in a hand. The product name can land in second two or three.
- Each proof is one shot: one action, one visible result. Three proofs beat twelve features.
- Scale must be readable. Hands, a known object, or the package in frame. Do not crop off the part the buyer uses (port, cap, blade, clasp, label).
- Texture and edges get a slower shot or a freeze. Speed ramps are for the approach, not for the frame where the viewer reads the label.
- Claims on screen are only claims in the brief or visible in the footage. Unknown specs stay unknown.
- Length: 15–30 seconds for a paid social ad, 20–45 seconds for an organic product post, longer only if each extra second shows a new proof. Do not pad to a platform maximum.

### UGC

- It should feel recorded, not templated. One place, one person, one product.
- Face or hands in the first two seconds. The creator talks to one person, not to “you guys.”
- Jump cuts on finished thoughts. Cutting every few words looks like a template.
- Native caption look: high contrast, plain, large enough to read on a phone. Trailer fonts and heavy glows mark the clip as an ad in the worst way.
- A small flaw in delivery is fine. A false result, a silent product swap, or music the brand cannot use is not.
- Pattern that holds: hook line, “I tried,” one proof the camera can see, one honest limit, one ask.

### Information video

- Open with the question or the result, then the steps. Do not open with the creator’s biography.
- One idea per card. The card is a sentence, not a paragraph. If the viewer must pause to read, the card is too long or the shot is too short.
- B-roll shows the noun that was just said. If the line is “tighten the cap,” the picture is the cap, not a stock sky.
- Diagrams get enough time to be read. Hold them. A 16:9 YouTube cut can hold longer than the 9:16 cut of the same lesson.
- For a lesson that must live in both places, finish the 16:9 master, then make a 9:16 cut that keeps the same claims. Do not reframe a diagram so the labels fall outside the vertical frame.
- Lists: number them, and say the number. “Three ways” with two shown is a failed info video.

## Workflows

Do the brief before the timeline. If the brief is missing a field, ask once, or mark it unknown and do not invent it.

### Shared brief

Write this down before New project:

1. Type: product, UGC, or info.
2. Viewer and the one action (buy, tap, watch next, remember a step).
3. Proof you actually have: files, photos, claims that are allowed.
4. Destination: organic, paid, or both, and which platforms.
5. Ratio and length.
6. Voice: on-camera, recorded voiceover, text-to-speech, or mute-with-captions.
7. Music: original, licensed file, or none. CapCut library music is not the default for ads. See Audio.

### A. Product video, brief to export

1. **Canvas.** Desktop, New project. Set 9:16 at 1080×1920 for TikTok, Reels, Shorts, and vertical ads. Set 16:9 at 1920×1080 only for a YouTube watch-page or landscape ad. Set 1:1 only when the placement is square. Set the ratio before the fine cut so you see what the phone will hide.
2. **Import.** Import only the provided takes, stills, and logo. Drag the selects to the timeline or use Import. You can also pull from Space or the stock library. Stock is not a substitute for the real product.
3. **Selects.** Watch once at full speed. Add a marker (the timeline control is **Add marker**) at hook, each proof, and the ask. Delete anything that does not show the product, the result, or the ask.
4. **Assembly, main track.** Order: hook, product identity, proof, proof, proof, ask. Cut with **Split**, then **Delete left** or **Delete right** to drop the dead side. Hard cuts. Freeze (**Freeze**) only on a result frame you want the eye to read.
5. **Layers.** Keep the main story on the main track. Put inserts and pack shots above it. Put a keyed person above the background that should show through. See Background removal.
6. **Type.** Product name once, readable, inside the safe zone. Price, offer, and CTA only if the brief gives them. Match the spoken line.
7. **Captions.** Generate auto captions from the voice, then correct the product name, numbers, and any word the recognizer ate. See Auto captions.
8. **Grade.** Neutral first: the product color must survive. Use Adjustment (Basic, HSL, Curves) and the color wheel if it is on screen. A filter is a last touch, not the grade. See Color.
9. **Sound.** Voice clear, music under it, one or two effects on actions (click, pour, latch). No library music on a paid ad unless the license covers that platform.
10. **Watch mute, then with sound, at phone size.** If the hook is unclear mute, recut the first second. If the label is unreadable, slow that shot or freeze it.
11. **Export** with the settings in Export. Run the copyright check when posting online. Do not press the TikTok or YouTube share button unless the brief says publish.

### B. UGC, brief to export

1. **Canvas.** 9:16, 1080×1920, Desktop. If the only footage is a phone screen recording of a CapCut mobile template, import that export and finish captions and claims on Desktop. Do not rebuild a trend template by guessing its effects.
2. **Performance pass.** Keep the take where the first line is a complete thought and the product is visible. Split out breaths and false starts with Delete left / Delete right. Leave a little air so it still sounds like a person.
3. **Face and product.** The face stays large enough to read. The product enters the hand or the frame before the claim about it. If auto reframe is used later, check that it did not crop the hand or the label.
4. **Captions in the creator’s language.** Generate, then fix slang, brand names, and punctuation. Place captions in the lower-middle safe area, not on the mouth and not under the platform buttons. Style: plain, thick, high contrast. Animations that bounce every word fight UGC; use a simple read.
5. **Pace.** Jump cut when a thought ends or a new proof starts. Do not run Auto Cut’s beat mode on a talking UGC unless the brief is a montage. Beat cuts on speech look accidental.
6. **Graphics.** One sticker or arrow only if it points at something the viewer would otherwise miss. Skip body effects, heavy lens effects, and stacked transitions.
7. **Voice.** Prefer the recorded take. Text-to-speech is a scratch track or an accessibility pass, not a fake creator. If you record in CapCut, use **Record** at the playhead (see Voiceover).
8. **Export** 1080×1920. Same mute test. The first on-screen line must make sense alone.

### C. Information video, brief to export

1. **Script.** One sentence per step. Mark which sentence needs a diagram, a screen, or a talking head. If there is no script, write a short one from the source material and keep unknown steps unknown.
2. **Two canvases if needed.** Build 16:9 (1920×1080) when the destination is a YouTube watch page. Duplicate the project for a 9:16 Shorts or Reels cut. Do not rely on one auto reframe of a dense diagram.
3. **Picture.** Talking head or title card states the question. Each step gets a visual that shows that step. Text templates can carry the step title; the body of the step is spoken and captioned, not typed as a block.
4. **Pacing.** Desktop: hold a diagram long enough to read it aloud once. Vertical cut: shorten the hold only if the words still fit in the caption. Speed curves are for dull setup, not for the frame that teaches.
5. **Captions and labels.** Auto captions on the voice track. Correct terms. Speaker labels only if the desktop build shows speaker detection and more than one person talks. On-screen labels must not sit on the diagram’s own text.
6. **Chapter energy without fake chapters.** A full-screen step number is enough. Do not add a progress bar, a lower third, and a caption that all repeat the same words.
7. **Clarify audio.** Noise reduction and Enhance voice if those checkboxes are visible and the room is bad. Then listen. Over-processed voice sounds like a call center and hurts a lesson.
8. **Export.** YouTube master: follow YouTube’s upload recommendations in Platform specs. Vertical cut: 1080×1920. Same facts in both files.

## Tool and trick reference

Paths are for **CapCut Desktop** unless a line says mobile or web. Names come from CapCut pages fetched on 22 September 2026, mainly the 10 September 2026 desktop how-to, the 31 August 2026 green-screen article, the January–February 2026 help articles, and the desktop product page. Libraries get renamed. Match the live label.

### Project setup

- **New project**, then **Import**, or drag media onto the timeline. Import also reaches **Space** and stock. **Fact.**
- Set ratio and resolution to the destination before cutting. The product page says changing aspect ratio can keep the main subject in frame. The 10 September 2026 article also names **Auto reframe** and lists it among tools you can try but not export without Pro. **Conflict.** Use the ratio control that is visible. If Auto reframe shows a Pro badge or the export dialog blocks it, tell the human. After any reframe, scrub the whole clip: hands, labels, and captions are what it crops.
- **Smart search** by objects, dialogue, and people is a desktop product-page feature. Use it to find a spoken line or a product appearance instead of scrubbing blind. The click path was not named. Use the search field in the media area if it is there.
- **Add marker** on the timeline for hook, proofs, and the ask.
- Timeline tools named above the track: **Split**, **Delete left**, **Delete right**, **Delete**, **Add marker**, plus freeze, reverse, mirror, rotate, and resize. **Fact** (10 September 2026 article).
- **Practice.** Main track holds the story. Tracks above hold inserts, keys, and text. Separate audio tracks for voice, music, and effects so you can duck music without moving captions. If a **Main track magnet** or **Track magnet** toggle and an **Auto snapping** toggle are visible, magnet on the main track is what closes a gap after a delete. Overlay tracks can keep a gap. Confirm by deleting a test gap on a copy, not by assuming a ripple-delete shortcut. A third-party shortcut list disagrees with CapCut’s own shortcut video; those extra shortcuts are not used here.

**Shortcuts, defaults, customizable.** CapCut’s own desktop shortcut video shows a Shortcuts panel at the top right. Remapping is allowed, so if a key does nothing, read that panel.

From that video, Mac defaults, with Control in place of Command on Windows for the Command keys:

- New project: Command+N
- Import: Command+I
- Full screen: Command+Control+F, exit with Escape. The video did not show a separate Windows fullscreen chord. Do not invent one. Use the fullscreen control or Escape.
- Split the selected clip: Command+B. Split all: Command+Shift+B
- Blade: B, then click a clip to split. A returns to select. (A later unofficial table says V for select. Do not use V unless the Shortcuts panel in this install says V.)
- Delete the clip side left of the playhead: Q. Right side: W
- Bracket keys move the selection. The video transcript cuts off the exact bracket pair. Read the panel before relying on them.

**Fact.** CapCut’s subtitle-split help says split is Ctrl+B on Windows and Cmd+B on macOS, or right-click **Split**. It fails if the playhead is outside the clip, if the clip is inside a group (right-click **Ungroup**), or if the piece is extremely short (that page says about 0.2–0.3 seconds). Zoom with scroll plus Ctrl or Command.

**Fact.** A 21 January 2026 help page says you can export a range with Shift+X, or with I for the In point and O for the Out point, in either order. Verify those three keys in the Shortcuts panel before you use them. They were not in the shortcut video transcript used here.

### Timeline and cuts

- Split on the frame where the action completes, or where a blink hides the join.
- **Delete left** and **Delete right** are the fast way to top-and-tail a talking take. Q and W do the same job when the shortcut map is still default.
- Do not reverse a clip (**Reverse**) to fake a result. Reverse is a visible trick. Use it only when the backward motion is the idea.
- **Freeze** on a label, a before/after, or a diagram. Do not freeze on a blink.
- If split will not cut a caption or a clip, check selection, playhead, group, and length, in that order.

### Pacing and speed

- **Fact.** **Speed > Curve** offers presets named bullet, montage, jump cut, hero time, flash in, and flash out, plus a custom curve (10 September 2026 article).
- **Practice.** One speed idea per video. A short ease into the product, then normal speed while the viewer reads. Do not ramp every cut.
- Jump-cut preset is not a substitute for real jump cuts on a UGC line. Use it for a montage.
- Match export frame rate to the source. A 30 fps phone clip exported at 60 fps does not become smoother. A 60 fps clip can stay 60 if the destination accepts it and motion is the point (hands, liquid, a latch). Lessons and talking UGC can stay at the source rate, usually 30 or the camera’s 24/25/30.

### Keyframes

- **Fact.** Desktop advertises keyframes and graphs. The how-to says to click the keyframe icon next to the property you are changing, on the right panel, and to animate position, scale, and similar values. A PC help page says the diamond sits next to parameters such as Position, Scale, or Opacity under Video, Transform, or Adjust, and that some template or grouped items cannot be keyframed.
- **Practice.** Two keyframes is a move. A third is only for a change of direction. Start the move slightly before the viewer needs to look, and finish before the next cut. Do not keyframe scale so far that the picture goes soft.
- Graphs are advertised. The pages read for this skill do not name the graph editor’s controls. Open a graph only if the keyframe row shows one. Do not invent easing names.

### Masks

- **Fact.** Right panel **Video > Mask** isolates an area (10 September 2026 article). Custom background removal uses a brush and eraser (31 August 2026 article, under custom removal).
- **Practice.** Mask the label to grade it, or mask a corner to hide a messy edge. Feather the mask edge. A hard oval over a face reads as a sticker. If the subject moves, a still mask will slip; track it or keyframe the mask only if that property shows a keyframe control.

### Tracking

CapCut’s articles describe more than one tracker. Use the one on screen. Do not combine the steps into a tool that is not there.

- **Fact.** One article says select the clip, open **Tracking**, set direction to forward, backward, or both, turn on Scale and Distance, place the yellow box, and start. It also says you can copy the clip onto a higher track before tracking.
- **Fact.** Another CapCut article says **Video > Camera tracking** under Basic, with subject choices such as face, body, hand, or a custom area.
- **Fact.** The 10 September 2026 article lists camera tracking among Pro-gated tools: copy the clip, paste it above, **Video > Motion tracking**, set direction, enable scale and distance. It says Pro tools can be previewed and then block export without a subscription.
- **Practice.** Track text or a callout to a moving part only when the part stays sharp and unobstructed. If the box slips, shorten the clip and track the good span, or drop the tracker and keyframe two positions by hand. A bad track is worse than static type.

### Background removal, chroma key, cutout

Three different jobs. Pick from the footage, not from habit.

| Footage | Tool | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Even green or blue screen | Chroma key | Color picker, then edge controls |
| No screen, a clear person or object | Auto removal / Auto cutout | AI matte. Edges of hair, hands, and motion need a frame-by-frame look |
| Awkward edge, glass, or a bad auto matte | Custom removal | Brush and eraser |

**Conflict, same company, two labels.** The 31 August 2026 green-screen article says **Video > Remove BG**, then **Chroma key**, **Auto removal**, or **Customized removal**. Strength, Shadow, and edge sliders clean the chroma key. The 10 September 2026 how-to says **Video > Cutout > Auto cutout** and **Chroma key**. Use Remove BG or Cutout, whichever is visible.

**Fact.** Put the replacement background on a track under the keyed clip so the hole shows the new scene. Preview at the cut, not only on a still. Green that matches clothing will eat the clothing. Wrinkled or uneven screens key badly; the article says to shoot a flat, even, bright screen and keep the screen color off the subject. Fix spill with the shadow and edge controls before you hide the edge with a blur.

**Practice.** For a product on a table, do not key the product out and drop it on a fake room. Shoot the product, or cut out a still only for a clean pack shot, and say that you did. Check the matte on fast hand motion. Export a short test before you finish a five-minute composite.

Green-screen templates: the article says search **green screen** under **Templates** and use **Replace**. Templates are often mobile-first. On Desktop, if Templates is absent, build the stack yourself.

### Auto captions

**Fact.** As of the 30 January 2026 help article, auto captions (also called recognise subtitles) exist on web, desktop, and mobile.

- Desktop: left toolbar **Captions > Auto Captions**, choose the audio source and language, **Generate**. Font, animation, and, in some regions, speaker detection.
- Web: **Captions > Auto Captions > Recognise**, language, **Generate**.
- Mobile: **Captions > Auto Captions**, language, **Generate**.
- Speaker differentiation, punctuation cleanup, and batch .srt export may be desktop or web only, and can depend on region and version. If .srt is not in the share or export sheet, do not claim you exported one.

**Practice.** Always read every line. Fix product names, numbers, homophones, and missing negatives (“not waterproof” dropped to “waterproof” is a serious error). One or two lines, high contrast, inside the safe zone, timed to the phrase. Split a long caption at the breath (Ctrl+B / Cmd+B on the caption clip). Do not cover the mouth in UGC or the diagram in a lesson.

The how-to puts preset text styles under left-panel **Text**, separate from the Captions tool. Captions are the transcript. Text is titles and callouts.

### Text and templates

- **Fact.** Left panel **Text** for presets, effects, and templates (10 September 2026).
- **Practice.** One type family for titles, the caption style for speech. Titles hold still long enough to read twice as fast as you can say them. Do not put the CTA in a moving template that never rests.
- Template text that cannot be keyframed: the keyframe help page says some template items will not animate. Detach or retype the line as a normal text clip if you must move it.
- Pro or premium templates show a badge and can block export. Remove them if the brief is a free-account delivery.

### Effects

- **Fact.** Left panel **Effects**. The 10 September 2026 article names categories including trending, nature, spark, love, lens, retro, and distortion, plus body effects. Categories change.
- **Practice.** Zero effects until the cut works. Then at most one, in service of the idea (a subtle lens on a dreamy UGC is already a lot). Body effects and distortion fight product truth. Delete any effect that changes the product’s shape or color.

### Transitions

- **Fact.** Left panel **Transitions**, with categories the article names as MG, blur, split, slide, and mask.
- **Practice.** Default is a cut. If you use a transition, use one type, short, at a real change of place or time. Do not put a transition on a jump cut in speech. The viewer hears the jump and the picture smears.

### Filters

- **Fact.** Left panel **Filters**, with groups the article names as mono, retro, style, food, and night scene.
- **Practice.** Filters are a look on top of a grade, not a fix for bad exposure. On product video, skip food and night filters unless the product is that scene. Compare the product color to the still the brand uses. If they disagree, remove the filter.

### Color

- **Fact.** Desktop product page: color wheel and auto adjust. The how-to puts color under right-panel **Adjustment**: **Basic** (brightness, contrast, and related), **HSL**, and **Curves**. **Relight** is named as a checkbox for uneven light. **Removing video flickers** is named under Video > Basic, inside the article’s Pro section.
- **Practice.** Order: exposure and white balance, then contrast, then HSL only on a cast you can see, then curves if a range is blocked, then a filter at low strength or not at all. Auto adjust is a starting read. If it shifts the product color, undo it.
- Skin and pack shots: keep skin off gray and keep the pack shot close to the real object. A teal-orange grade on a skincare product is a different product.
- If Relight or flicker removal shows a Pro lock, skip it and say so. Do not relight so hard that shadows on the product disappear; buyers use shadows to read shape.

### Audio

- **Fact.** Volume and fades, extract audio, noise reduction. Right panel **Audio**: **Loudness normalization**, **Noise reduction**. **Enhance voice** with an intensity slider. **Vocal isolation** (keep or remove vocals) is listed with the Pro tools in the 10 September 2026 article.
- **Practice.** Voice is the loudest intelligible element, not the loudest waveform. Duck music under speech by lowering the music clip, not by crushing the voice with Enhance. Use noise reduction on a noisy room, then A/B it. If the voice turns watery, back it off.
- Loudness: normalize if the checkbox is there, then listen on a phone speaker. Desktop meters and phone speakers disagree.
- Separate tracks: voice, music, sound effects. Mute a track to test the others.

**Music license. Fact, and it overrides marketing copy.** The 10 September 2026 article calls the in-app songs royalty-free. CapCut’s Materials License Agreement, fetched 22 September 2026, does not. It says **Commercial Sounds** may be used by commercial users in videos shown on CapCut, TikTok, and TikTok for Business. Uses on other platforms need a separate license from the rights holders. Ordinary sounds are not that commercial library. Region versions of the agreement differ slightly in who the contract names. Read the license in the signed-in region.

**Practice.** For Reels, Shorts, YouTube, Meta ads, or any non-TikTok paid placement, do not drop a CapCut Commercial Sound and assume it is cleared. Use the brand’s track, a track with a written license for that platform, or original audio. Turn on **Run a copyright check** in the export panel when that toggle is there (named in the 10 September 2026 article) before anything goes online. YouTube’s own Shorts help adds another limit: many Shorts Audio Library tracks are usable only up to 90, 60, or 30 seconds inside a Short of up to 3 minutes. A claim can still attach.

### Music and beat sync

- **Fact.** **Auto Cut** (3 February 2026 and the companion troubleshooting article): desktop and mobile, not web. It can follow music beats, speech pauses, or, where the rollout has reached that account, a text instruction. Desktop article path: **AI** on the top toolbar, or **Edit > AI Tools > Auto Cut**, then Beat Sync, Speech Pause Detection, or AI Script Mode, then Apply or Generate. The troubleshooting article says desktop Auto Cut arrived around version 5.0 and can be missing by region, and that mobile needs a recent app (it names 12.0 or later) under **Templates > AI Creation**, sometimes labeled Beat Cut, Rhythm Edit, or Smart Trim. It may be hidden inside an existing template or a collaborative project. Text commands are a gradual rollout. If the AI button is absent, do not invent a chat cut.
- **Practice.** Beat sync is for montages and product rhythm cuts, not for a sentence. After it generates, delete every cut that lands on a label, a blink, or a half-word. Then mark the beats you kept and turn the rest back into a performance. Sensitivity and minimum clip length, if those sliders exist, should leave the product on screen long enough to recognize.

### Voiceover and text-to-speech

- **Fact.** **Record**: put the playhead where the voice should start, click **Record**, speak, click again to stop. A new audio track appears. Voice-change examples named in the how-to FAQ: echo, deep, tremble, synth.
- **Fact.** Text-to-speech needs a text layer, a chosen voice and language, and **Generate**. CapCut’s TTS help says it is cloud-processed, so it fails offline, on an unsupported language, or when no text is selected. The generated clip can also be muted or sitting off the timeline. Desktop, web, and mobile all have a TTS path in that article; the label is **Text-to-speech**.
- **Practice.** Record the real read when a person is the brand. Use TTS for a scratch timing pass, a mute-friendly info bed, or a language you cannot record. Do not use TTS as a fake customer. Do not use a voice changer to imitate a real person. After TTS, captions must match the generated words exactly, including mistakes you choose to keep or fix in the text and regenerate.

### Stickers

- **Fact.** Left panel **Stickers**, including an **AI generated** tab where you describe a sticker (10 September 2026).
- **Practice.** A sticker must point, label, or react. One is usually enough. Animated stickers behind captions make both unreadable. AI stickers that depict the product can invent parts the product does not have. Do not use them as product truth.

### Auto reframe

- **Fact.** The desktop product page says you can change aspect ratio and keep the main subject in frame. The how-to names **Auto reframe**, including stabilization and camera-move speed, and lists it with Pro export locks.
- **Practice.** Reframe talking heads and single products. Do not auto-reframe a two-person scene, a labeled diagram, or a pack shot with small type and then ship it. Watch the whole clip once in the new ratio. Rebuild the vertical as its own timeline when the horizontal composition is the lesson.

**Conflict on Pro.** Pages do not agree, so the open export panel is the rule.

- 14 January 2026 export help: Desktop can choose 2K (2560×1440) or 4K (3840×2160), frame rate up to 60 fps depending on source, and bitrate. It says free **web** accounts may get watermarks or bitrate limits on 4K, and Pro gets unrestricted web 4K. It does not say desktop 4K is always locked.
- 2 April 2026 “why am I asked to buy Pro” help: examples of Pro include 4K/60 fps export, watermark removal, AI generation, exclusive templates, premium effects, and expanded cloud storage. It also says export options include up to 4K and 60 fps, MOV or MP4, codec, and bit rate, without calling 4K Pro in that paragraph.

### Export

**Fact.** Export is the top-right **Export** button in the 2026 help and how-to, not a guessed File menu. The how-to says the panel can set cover, title, destination, frame rate up to 60 fps, resolution up to 4K, format MOV or MP4, codec, and bit rate. **Export audio** can write MP3, WAV, or FLAC. **Run a copyright check** is a toggle. Quick share to TikTok and YouTube is in that panel. The article tells you to set 9:16 for TikTok and 16:9 for YouTube. That YouTube line is about a landscape share. A Short is vertical. Do not flip a Short to 16:9 because of that sentence.

**Practice, delivery default for this user.**

- Vertical organic and paid social: 1080×1920, same frame rate as the timeline, MP4, H.264 if the codec list offers it, AAC if an audio codec is offered.
- YouTube watch-page master: 1920×1080, MP4, H.264, AAC-LC, 48 kHz, progressive, Rec.709 / SDR unless the whole chain is HDR. YouTube’s published SDR bitrates for 1080p are 8 Mbps at 24/25/30 fps and 12 Mbps at 48/50/60. For 2160p, 35–45 Mbps (standard frame rate) or 53–68 Mbps (high frame rate). Those figures are on YouTube’s upload-encoding page. CapCut’s bitrate field may be labeled Kbps or Mbps or only Recommended / Higher. Read the unit. 8 Mbps is 8000 Kbps. If the panel has no number, choose the higher of Recommended and whatever still looks clean in a local playback test.
- Do not export 4K from 1080p sources. You are enlarging, not adding detail. If 4K is locked, export the best unlocked resolution and say so.
- Color: leave ordinary SDR at Rec.709 if a color-space control exists. Do not switch on HDR at export unless the source and the destination are both HDR.
- Set In and Out (I / O or Shift+X, if the Shortcuts panel confirms them) when you only need a section.
- Watch the exported file, not only the preview. Preview playback can be softer than the file. If a preview-quality control is visible and lowered, do not “fix” the grade to compensate.
- Do not click Share unless the brief says to post.

**Mobile export. Fact (14 January 2026).** The resolution chip beside Export can list 720p, 1080p, 2K, or 4K depending on the phone, the source resolution, region, and version. You cannot export 4K from a timeline whose media is all below 4K. High frame rate can hide high resolutions. Many Android phones stop at 1080p or 2K.

**Web export. Fact (same article).** 1080p, 2K, and 4K can appear when the browser can encode, the connection can render in the cloud, and the media is at least that resolution. Chrome on Windows or macOS is the combination that article calls most reliable. Free accounts may be watermarked or bitrate-limited at 4K.

## Platform specs and safe zones

Design one vertical master at 1080×1920 when the job is TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Upload a larger file only when that ad spec asks for it. Percentages below are the platform’s, applied to whatever canvas you actually export. Pixel math on 1080×1920 is practice derived from those percentages, not a second official table.

### TikTok

**Organic length.** Do not guess a maximum. The in-app camera and the upload sheet show the limit for this account, and it has changed by region and by rollout. Confirm it there.

**Paid, auction in-feed. Fact.** TikTok Ads Manager help, last updated June 2026:

- Spark Ads pulled from an organic post: .mp4 or .mov, duration not restricted by that section, caption taken from the organic caption (up to 4 lines shown).
- Non-Spark: vertical 9:16 recommended, at least 540×960; horizontal 16:9 at least 960×540; square 1:1 at least 640×640. Formats .mp4, .mov, .mpeg, .3gp, .avi. Duration up to 10 minutes. File up to 500 MB. Bitrate at least 516 Kbps. Caption is white, not customizable, and cannot contain links, @, or hashtags.
- Safe zone: TikTok does not publish one pixel inset. It depends on vertical, horizontal, or square, on caption length, and on extras such as an anchor. Download the template: standard LTR for non-Arabic regions, Arabic RTL for the Arabic region, and the “with anchor” file if the ad uses an anchor. The preview can differ from the live ad. Keep the product, the claim, and the face inside the template’s clear area, and keep them out of the right-hand buttons and the bottom caption.

**Practice.** A 1080×1920, H.264 MP4, at least the 516 Kbps floor and preferably a much higher rate so the file survives their recompress, is the working export. 516 Kbps is a minimum, not a quality target.

### Instagram and Facebook Reels

**Organic. Fact.** Facebook Help on reel size: upload aspect ratio from 1.91:1 to 9:16, minimum 30 fps, minimum 720 pixels. Cover recommendation on that page: 420×654 (about 1:1.55), with a note that the cover could not be edited after upload. Treat the cover note as something to recheck in the upload screen; help pages lag the app.

**Fact.** Instagram Help on recording a reel (fetched 22 September 2026): you can record up to 20 minutes. Reels over 3 minutes are not recommended to new audiences. So 3 minutes is a distribution warning, not the record limit on that page.

**Paid Reels. Fact.** Meta’s Facebook Reels awareness video spec: MP4, MOV, or GIF; 9:16 recommended; H.264, square pixels, fixed frame rate, progressive, stereo AAC above 128 kbps; resolution 1440×2560; captions recommended; sound strongly recommended; primary text about 40 characters; headline about 55; file up to 4 GB; no maximum duration on that page. Safe zone: leave about 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom, and 6% of each side free of text, logos, and other key elements. Instagram Reels image guidance in the Ads Guide uses the same 14% / 35% / 6% inset.

**Practice on a 1080×1920 master.** 14% top is about 269 pixels, 35% bottom is about 672 pixels, 6% of the width is about 65 pixels each side. Put claims inside the remaining center. If the ad carries a disclaimer, open Meta’s current safe-zone note in Ads Manager; do not assume 35% is still enough. A 9:16 reel can also be cropped when it appears in the main feed. Keep the product in the center, not only in the top or bottom third.

### YouTube

**Shorts. Fact.** YouTube Help: a square or vertical video up to 3 minutes is a Short (standard channels: uploads on or after 15 October 2024; the page states a later date, 8 December 2025, for Official Artist Channels). To stay off the Shorts shelf, upload 16:9. Upload in the YouTube app or YouTube Studio. Music: Shorts Audio Library, with many tracks limited to 90, 60, or 30 seconds, or the YouTube Audio Library. From 24 September 2026, new Shorts between 1 and 3 minutes with a Content ID claim are not automatically blocked the way they were; a claim can still attach. Read the current music-eligibility page before you rely on a commercial track.

**Watch page.** 16:9, 1920×1080 recommended. YouTube’s encoding page (it is written for Content Manager partners, and it is still YouTube’s published recommendation): MP4, H.264, AAC-LC or Opus, stereo 48 kHz, progressive, closed GOP, 4:2:0, Rec.709 for SDR. Match the recorded frame rate. Bitrates as listed in Export above. The player letterboxes other ratios; it does not turn a vertical video into a good 16:9 lesson.

**YouTube ads. Fact.** Google Ads video specs: recommended 1080p pixels are 1920×1080, 1080×1920, or 1080×1080. Minimum discussed there is 720p. File size up to 256 GB. Preferred container in that table is .MPG, and MP4 is in the accepted list. Safe zones are downloadable images, not a sentence of percentages. Keep logo, product, and supers inside the red area on Google’s vertical, square, and horizontal templates. Overlays move by format, campaign, and screen.

**Shorts ads. Fact.** Google’s Shorts ads page: vertical 9:16 is what they recommend; landscape and square can run but vertical is preferred for Shorts in brand campaigns. Ads may be up to 3 minutes, but only the first 60 seconds play in the Shorts feed, and an overlay at about 50 seconds offers the rest on the watch page. They recommend under 60 seconds. Action-oriented: 10–30 second vertical. Video Reach: 6–60 seconds. Other named campaign types: 10–60 seconds. The video must be on YouTube (public or unlisted) before it can be a Shorts ad.

### Paid versus organic

Build the paid safe zone first if the same file will be boosted. Organic buttons still cover the right edge and the bottom caption, so a paid-safe layout usually survives organic. The reverse is not true.

## Quality checklist

Run this on the exported file, muted first, then with sound, once at full frame and once imagining a phone.

- The first second shows the product, the result, or the question.
- The promised action is the last thing, and it is the only ask.
- Every on-screen claim is in the brief or visible in the shot.
- Captions match speech, including “not” and the product name.
- Captions, logo, and CTA sit inside the safe zone for the strictest destination.
- The product’s shape, color, and parts match the real item.
- No Pro badge left on the timeline if the export must succeed on this account.
- No CapCut Commercial Sound if the destination is outside CapCut, TikTok, and TikTok for Business, unless a separate license is in the brief.
- Voice is intelligible on a phone speaker. Music does not cover the product name.
- Cuts do not flash a half-word, a blink used as a mistake, or a smear from a transition.
- Key, mask, and tracker edges were scrubbed, not spot-checked on one frame.
- Frame rate matches the source. Resolution is not an upscale of a smaller file.
- Copyright check was run if that toggle exists and the file will be posted.
- The local file plays. The share button was not pressed unless posting was requested.

## Common failures and fixes

| What you see | What is going on | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Split does nothing | Playhead outside the clip, clip not selected, or the item is grouped | Select the clip, park the playhead inside it, Ungroup if needed, or press the on-screen Split. Check the shortcut map if Ctrl+B / Cmd+B is dead |
| A gap after a delete | The clip was not on the magnetized main track | Move the story to the main track, or close the gap by sliding the later clips. Confirm the magnet toggle if it is labeled |
| Green fringe or holes in clothes | Chroma color matches the subject, or Strength is too high | Resample a clean screen color, lower Strength, use Shadow and edge, or rotoscope the bad frames with custom removal |
| Auto matte shimmers on hair or hands | Auto removal is the wrong tool for that edge | Shorten the keyed span, or switch that span to chroma or a mask |
| Captions say the wrong product or drop a “not” | Recognizer error | Edit the text. Do not ship |
| Text under the like button or the caption bar | Safe zone ignored | Move type into the center using the platform template. Rebuild; do not scale the whole video down with black bars unless the brief asks for a frame |
| Auto reframe cuts off the label | Subject tracking followed the face or the center of mass | Keyframe position by hand, or cut a vertical version |
| Export asks for Pro | A premium asset, 4K, watermark removal, or a listed Pro tool | Remove the asset or pick an unlocked resolution. Do not buy from the editor. Report the exact banner sentence |
| 4K missing on desktop | OS, GPU, source resolution, or plan lock. Pages disagree | Read About, read the export dialog, export the best unlocked size |
| 4K missing on a phone | Source is below 4K, the phone is mid-range, or frame rate is high | Export 1080p. That is normal on many Android phones |
| TTS silent | Offline, wrong language, text not selected, or the clip is muted or out of range | Fix that cause and generate again |
| Auto Cut missing | Web, old version, region rollout, or you are inside a template | Edit on Desktop at a current version, or cut by hand. Do not switch to web to find it |
| Track slips off the product | Box too big, or the object leaves frame | Track a shorter span, tighten the box, or keyframe |
| Voice pumps or sounds underwater | Enhance voice or noise reduction too high | Lower the slider or turn it off. Normalize loudness instead of stacking both |
| Music claim or a block on YouTube | Library license or Shorts Audio Library length | Replace the music. Commercial Sounds are not a YouTube license |
| Product color shifts | Filter, auto adjust, or Relight | Bypass them and grade Basic only until the pack matches |
| Soft export | Preview was soft, or bitrate was at the TikTok floor, or the source was small | Judge the file. Raise bitrate using the unit the panel shows. Do not upscale |
| Share posted the wrong ratio | YouTube quick share assumed 16:9 | Export the file yourself. Upload a Short as 9:16 |

## How an agent operates the app

### When you can click the UI

Do this in order. After each step, look at the screen before the next click.

1. Confirm the front window is **CapCut Desktop on Windows**, not the phone app and not the browser editor, unless this skill told you to use mobile or web for a named gap.
2. Read the brief using the shared brief list. If a claim or a file is missing, stop and name it. Do not browse the web for a product spec to fill the hole.
3. New project. Set the ratio and the working resolution for the destination before you cut.
4. Import only the media you were given. If you use stock, a template, or an AI generate, say so in the handoff and keep it out of product-truth shots.
5. Watch the selects. Add markers for hook, proofs, and the ask.
6. Assemble on the main track with Split, Delete left, and Delete right. Add layers only when a layer has a job (insert, key, title, caption, voice, music).
7. Apply tools from this skill in this order: cut, then captions, then type, then key or mask or track, then color, then audio, then one effect or transition if it still earns its place. Skip any step that does not apply.
8. For every Pro badge or timeline banner, remove that item or pause and report the banner text. Do not click Upgrade, Join, or Subscribe.
9. Play mute from the start. Then play with sound. Scrub every key, tracker, and caption at the frames where the subject moves fastest.
10. Export with the delivery default. Turn on copyright check if it is there. Play the file from disk.
11. Do not press Share, Post, or Schedule.
12. Report: file path, ratio, frame rate, codec if shown, duration, what you corrected in captions, any tool you could not find, any Pro lock, and any claim you refused to add.

If a label in this skill is not on screen, write down the app version from About and the labels you do see in that panel. Stop that step. Do not click a similar icon and hope.
